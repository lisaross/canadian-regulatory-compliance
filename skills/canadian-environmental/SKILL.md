# Canadian Environmental Compliance

## Key Legislation

| Legislation | Jurisdiction | Scope |
|-------------|-------------|-------|
| Canadian Environmental Protection Act (CEPA 1999), as amended by Bill S-5 (2023) | Federal | Toxic substances, right to a healthy environment, pollution prevention, environmental emergencies, Watch List |
| Impact Assessment Act (IAA), as amended June 2024 | Federal | Federal environmental assessment; "designated projects" scheme narrowed by 2023 SCC 23; Building Canada Act overlay for national interest projects |
| Greenhouse Gas Pollution Pricing Act (GGPPA) | Federal | Industrial carbon pricing (OBPS); consumer fuel charge removed effective April 1, 2025 and repealed by Bill C-4 (2026) |
| Transportation of Dangerous Goods Act (TDG) | Federal | Classification, handling, and transport of dangerous goods |
| Fisheries Act | Federal | Protection of fish and fish habitat; prohibition on harmful deposits |
| Species at Risk Act (SARA) | Federal | Protection of listed wildlife species and their habitats |
| Ontario EPA (Environmental Protection Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. E.19) | Ontario | Provincial environmental approvals, spills, waste |
| BC Environmental Management Act (EMA) | BC | Provincial discharge permits, contaminated sites, waste |
| Alberta Environmental Protection and Enhancement Act (EPEA) | Alberta | Provincial approvals, waste, contaminated sites |

## Carbon Pricing in Canada

Carbon pricing in Canada is now **industrial-only** — the federal consumer fuel charge has been eliminated. Provinces/territories can run their own industrial system — if it meets federal benchmarks, the federal OBPS backstop does not apply.

### Federal Carbon Pricing: Industrial Only (as of September 2026)

**1. Fuel Charge (consumer-facing) — REMOVED**
- Rates set to $0 effective **April 1, 2025** by regulation.
- Bill C-4, *Making Life More Affordable for Canadians Act* (Royal Assent **March 12, 2026**), permanently removed the fuel charge from federal legislation.
- The requirement for provinces and territories to maintain a consumer-facing carbon price was also removed as of April 1, 2025.
- The **Canada Carbon Rebate for individuals ended** — no quarterly payments after April 2025. The Canada Carbon Rebate for Small Businesses made its final payment for the 2024-25 fuel charge year and was made non-taxable for all fuel charge years by legislation passed March 26, 2026.
- **Residual obligation:** registrants and non-registrants must still file outstanding returns and pay amounts owing for periods **before April 1, 2025**. Inspection, assessment, penalty, collection and confidentiality provisions continue to apply until **April 1, 2035**.

**2. Output-Based Pricing System (OBPS) — STILL IN FORCE**
- Applies to facilities that reported 50,000+ tonnes CO₂e/year (2014 or later) in a backstop jurisdiction listed in Part 2 of Schedule 1 to the GGPPA; facilities emitting 10,000+ tonnes in certain sectors may opt in (requests due July 15 for the following year).
- Registration must be applied for within 30 days of meeting the criteria.
- Facilities above the output-based standard compensate for excess emissions; facilities below earn surplus credits to sell or bank.
- Compensation: excess emissions charge payment, surplus credits, federal offset credits, or recognized provincial/territorial offset credits.
- **Federal OBPS backstop jurisdictions as of 2026:** Prince Edward Island, Manitoba, Yukon, Nunavut.

**Industrial carbon price trajectory — updated May 15, 2026**
- Announced benchmark trajectory (May 15, 2026) that provincial and federal industrial systems are expected to meet: $95 (2026), $100 (2027–2029), $115 (2030) … $140 (2040). This is the policy benchmark, not yet the enacted federal charge: GGPPA Schedule 4 still sets the federal OBPS excess-emissions charge at $110 for 2026 rising to $170 in 2030 until amended. Alberta's TIER fund price is separately frozen at $95. Confirm the operative rate for the specific system and compliance year.

### Provincial Carbon Pricing Systems (Meeting Federal Benchmark)

**British Columbia — Carbon Tax ELIMINATED; B.C. OBPS in force**
- B.C.'s consumer carbon tax (Canada's oldest, from 2008) was **eliminated effective April 1, 2025**. All carbon tax accounts are closed; the climate action tax credit was cancelled, final payment April 2025.
- Returns for periods before April 1, 2025 may still be amended, and audits for those periods continue.
- Large industry is covered by the **B.C. Output-Based Pricing System**, in force since **April 1, 2024** under the *Greenhouse Gas Industrial Reporting and Control Act* (GGIRCA). It replaced the CleanBC Industrial Incentive Program.
- Coverage: operations emitting **10,000+ tCO₂e/year** (opt-in below that). Tightening rate 1%/yr on the reduction factor (0% for industrial process emissions).
- Key dates: **May 31** reporting; **Nov 1** invoices; **Nov 30** compliance. Reporting via BCIERS.

**Alberta — TIER (Technology Innovation and Emissions Reduction)**
- Applies to facilities that emitted **100,000+ tonnes CO₂e** in 2016 or any subsequent year, or that import more than 10,000 tonnes of hydrogen annually.
- **Opt-in threshold is 2,000 tonnes CO₂e** (lowered from 10,000 in 2023) for EITE-sector facilities competing directly with a regulated facility.
- Uses **facility-specific** and **high-performance** benchmarks, not a flat sector average. Stringency tightens **2%/yr** from 2023 (1%/yr 2020–2022); oil sands mining, upgrading and in-situ tighten at **4%/yr in 2029–2030**; electricity uses a "good-as-best-gas" benchmark tightened 2%/yr.
- Compliance options: on-site reductions, emission performance credits, Alberta offset credits, TIER Fund payment, and — per the **December 2025 amendments (OC 369/2025)** — recognised **on-site direct investment** in emissions reduction projects.
- **TIER fund price frozen at $95/tonne indefinitely** since May 12, 2025 — no longer tracks the federal trajectory. Under the Canada–Alberta Implementation Agreement (May 15, 2026), the path rises slowly to **$140 in 2040**.
- Credit use limit: 90% of total compliance obligation from 2026 onward.

**Quebec — Cap-and-Trade (Western Climate Initiative)**
- Linked with California's cap-and-trade system
- Applies to industrial emitters, electricity importers, fuel distributors
- Companies must surrender allowances equal to their emissions

**Provincial vs. federal industrial coverage (2026):** Newfoundland & Labrador, Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Ontario, Saskatchewan, Alberta and British Columbia each run a **provincial** industrial carbon pricing system; Quebec runs cap-and-trade; the Northwest Territories runs a territorial system. The **federal OBPS** applies in Prince Edward Island, Manitoba, Yukon and Nunavut. Saskatchewan paused its industrial system effective April 1, 2025.

## Environmental Assessment

### Federal — Impact Assessment Act (IAA), as amended June 2024
The IAA replaced CEAA 2012. In **Reference re Impact Assessment Act, 2023 SCC 23** (October 13, 2023) the Supreme Court held the "designated projects" scheme *ultra vires* Parliament; ss. 81–91 (federal lands / federal funding / outside Canada) were upheld. Parliament responded through the *Budget Implementation Act, 2024*, and the **amended IAA came into force June 20, 2024**.

Key consequences:
- The Agency **cannot require an impact assessment unless satisfied the project may cause non-negligible adverse *federal* effects**. Other factors are considered only once that precondition is met.
- The final public-interest decision must be grounded in **significant adverse federal effects**; non-federal factors cannot aggravate them, though they may inform the positive side of the ledger.
- Governor in Council time-limit extensions are limited to **once, for a definite period**, with reasons posted to the Registry.
- Substitution: the Minister may substitute a process **in whole or in part** to another jurisdiction.

**Building Canada Act overlay (Bill C-5, RA June 26, 2025):** for projects added to Schedule 1 as "national interest projects", IAA ss. 9–17 and 18(3)–(6) do not apply; the Agency is deemed to have decided an assessment is required, and federal authorizations across Schedule 2 statutes are consolidated into a single **conditions document** issued by the Minister of One Canadian Economy. Target: federal review within 2 years. Treaty-based assessment processes are unaffected and must conclude before a conditions document issues.

**Designated projects include (selected):**
- Coal, diamond and metal mines and metal mills: 5,000+ tonnes/day (rare earth element mines: 2,500 t/day; quarries and sand/gravel pits: 3,500,000 t/year)
- Oil sands projects 10,000+ m³/day
- Pipelines (CER Act, other than offshore) requiring **75+ km of new right of way** — note this is *new right of way*, not total pipeline length
- All-season public highways requiring 75+ km of new right of way
- International electrical transmission lines of 345 kV+ requiring 75+ km of new right of way
- Nuclear facilities
- Offshore oil/gas projects
- Certain hydroelectric facilities, bridges

**IA process:**
1. Proponent submits Initial Project Description → Impact Assessment Agency of Canada (IAAC) screens for designation
2. Planning phase: IAAC identifies issues, indigenous consultation begins
3. Impact Assessment phase: proponent submits Impact Statement; public and expert review
4. Decision phase: Minister (or Governor in Council for major projects) makes final decision, grounded in significant adverse federal effects
5. Post-decision: conditions and follow-up monitoring

### Provincial Environmental Assessment Triggers

| Province | Legislation | Key Triggers |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Ontario | Environmental Assessment Act | Designated undertakings (transit, waste, hydro projects); Class EAs for routine projects |
| BC | Environmental Assessment Act | Projects in prescribed categories by size (mines, energy, water, waste, industrial) |
| Alberta | EPEA + Responsible Energy Development Act | Energy projects via AER; non-energy projects via AEP |

**Coordination:** Federal and provincial EAs may be conducted jointly to avoid duplication.

## Hazardous Waste and Dangerous Goods

### Transportation of Dangerous Goods (TDG)
The federal TDG Act regulates the transport of dangerous goods by road, rail, air, and water.

**Key obligations:**
- **Classification:** Classify materials using the TDG classification system (9 classes including explosives, flammable liquids, toxic substances, corrosives, etc.)
- **Shipping documents:** Provide accurate shipping documents identifying the dangerous goods
- **Labels and placards:** Apply required labels to containers and placards to vehicles
- **Emergency Response Assistance Plans (ERAPs):** Required for certain high-risk substances
- **Training:** Anyone who handles, offers for transport, or transports dangerous goods must be trained and certified
- **Emergency reporting:** Report accidents/incidents involving dangerous goods to CANUTEC (1-888-CANUTEC)

### Provincial Hazardous Waste (Manifesting)

| Province | System | Key Requirement |
|---------|--------|----------------|
| Ontario | Hazardous Waste Program Registry (RPRA) | Since **January 1, 2023**, generators, carriers and receivers register and report through the Resource Productivity and Recovery Authority's online registry, replacing the Ministry's HWIN system. Paper manifests ended; electronic manifesting via the registry and the RPRA HazTrack app. An emergency generator registration route exists for spill clean-up, which issues an Emergency Generator Number (EGN). Governed by amended O. Reg. 347. |
| BC | BC Hazardous Waste Regulation | Licensed carriers; manifest system; storage limits and facility approvals |
| Alberta | Hazardous Waste Regulation (EPEA) | Generator registration; manifest for transport; licensed disposal facilities only |

**Cradle-to-grave responsibility:** Generators remain responsible for hazardous waste until it is properly disposed of. Improper disposal can result in significant civil and regulatory liability.

## Spill Reporting Requirements

### Federal (Fisheries Act, s.38 / CEPA)
- **Immediate reporting** to Environment and Climate Change Canada if a spill of a polluting substance reaches or may reach a waterway
- Report to Transport Canada for TDG incidents involving dangerous goods

### Ontario (EPA, s.15)
- **Immediate reporting** to the Spills Action Centre (1-800-268-6060) for any spill that causes or is likely to cause an adverse effect
- Owner/operator must take immediate clean-up action
- Written report required within prescribed timeframe for significant spills

### BC (EMA + Spill Reporting Regulation, B.C. Reg. 187/2017)
- Under s. 4(1), a responsible person must **immediately report** any spill — or imminent risk of a spill — by calling **1-800-663-3456**
- That number reaches the **Emergency Coordination Centre (Emergency Management BC)**, which notifies the appropriate Environmental Emergency Response Officer
- Marine pollution in Canadian waters: Canadian Coast Guard, 1-800-889-8852

### Alberta (EPEA)
- **Immediate reporting** to the **Energy and Environmental 24-Hour Response Line**: 1-800-222-6514 (toll-free in Alberta) or 780-422-4505 from outside Alberta
- Reportable: contraventions of EPEA or *Water Act* authorizations; releases of a TDG-regulated substance above the reporting limit; any release with potential for adverse effects
- **Written report due within 7 days** to epa.writtenreports@gov.ab.ca

## CEPA Schedule 1 Substances

Bill S-5, *Strengthening Environmental Protection for a Healthier Canada Act* (Royal Assent **June 13, 2023**), made the first major amendments to CEPA since 1999:

- **Right to a healthy environment.** CEPA now recognises that every individual in Canada has a right to a healthy environment, and requires the government to protect it subject to reasonable limits, upholding environmental justice, non-regression and intergenerational equity.
- **Schedule 1 renamed and split.** The title "List of Toxic Substances" was removed. **Part 1** lists highest-risk substances (persistent + bioaccumulative + inherently toxic; carcinogenic, mutagenic or toxic for reproduction; or otherwise highest risk) — priority goes to total, partial or conditional **prohibition**. **Part 2** lists all other toxic substances — priority goes to **pollution prevention**.
- **Watch List** (s. 75.1) of substances suspected of being *capable of becoming toxic*. Substances come off it if added to Schedule 1 or if the suspicion no longer holds. Useful for avoiding regrettable substitutions.
- **Plan of Chemicals Management Priorities**, replacing the Priority Substances List, with published timelines. Assessments must consider vulnerable populations, vulnerable environments, and cumulative effects.
- **Any person may request** that the Ministers assess a substance; the Ministers must consider and respond.
- Virtual elimination provisions were repealed and replaced.

If your business manufactures, uses, imports, or releases a Schedule 1 substance:
- Determine whether it sits in **Part 1** (expect prohibition-oriented instruments) or **Part 2** (expect pollution prevention instruments)
- Check if a **Pollution Prevention (P2) Plan** is required
- Check for **Environmental Performance Agreements** or industry codes of practice
- Report under **NPRI** if the facility meets the employee/activity and substance thresholds
- Check the **Watch List** when selecting substitutes

## Penalties

Maximum fines vary by offence class, by mode of prosecution (indictment vs. summary conviction), by first vs. subsequent offence, and — federally — by whether the offender is an individual, a small-revenue corporation, or another corporation. Figures below are **maximums on indictment for a first offence** unless noted.

| Legislation | Individual (max) | Corporation (max) | Per day? |
|-------------|----------------|-----------------|----------|
| CEPA s. 272 (designated offences) | $1M (+ up to 3 yrs); $2M subsequent | $6M; $12M subsequent ($4M / $8M small-revenue) | No |
| Fisheries Act s. 40 | $1M; $2M subsequent (+ up to 3 yrs) | $6M; $12M subsequent ($4M / $8M small-revenue) | No |
| TDG Act s. 33 | Summary: $50K first, $100K each subsequent — same for any person. Indictment: up to 2 yrs imprisonment. | Same as individual — no corporate distinction | No |
| Ontario EPA s. 187 (ordinary) | $50K first; $100K subsequent | $250K first; $500K subsequent | **Yes** |
| Ontario EPA s. 187 (s. 186(3) serious) | $5K–$4M first; up to $6M subsequent (+ up to 5 yrs less a day) | $25K–$6M first; up to $10M second and subsequent | **Yes** |
| BC EMA s. 120 | Tiered by offence: $2K (littering), $200K, $300K, or $1M (+ up to 6 months). No individual/corporate distinction. | Same tiers | **Yes** (s. 122) |
| Alberta EPEA s. 228(1) — *knowing* offences | $100K (+ up to 2 yrs) | $1M | **Yes** (s. 231) |
| Alberta EPEA s. 228(2) — other specified offences | $50K | $500K | **Yes** (s. 231) |

**Beyond fines:** courts may order additional fines equal to any monetary benefit gained (CEPA s. 274.1, EPEA s. 230); creative sentencing, remediation and community service orders are common. Directors and officers who directed, authorized, assented to or acquiesced in an offence are personally liable (CEPA s. 280, EMA s. 121, EPEA s. 232) whether or not the corporation is prosecuted.

**Administrative monetary penalties** are a separate and far more frequently used track: CEPA AMPs under EVAMPA and the 2017 AMPs Regulations (Parts 7 and 9); Ontario environmental penalties up to $100,000/day; Alberta administrative penalties capped at **$5,000 per contravention per day** (and, under EPEA s. 237(3), an administrative penalty bars subsequent prosecution for the same contravention).

## Plastics and Fuels

### Single-use Plastics Prohibition Regulations (SOR/2022-138)
- **In force.** Prohibit the manufacture, import and sale of single-use plastic checkout bags, cutlery, foodservice ware made from or containing problematic plastics, ring carriers, stir sticks and straws (with a flexible-straw exception).
- **Litigation:** the Federal Court quashed the 2021 Order listing "plastic manufactured items" on Schedule 1 in November 2023 (2023 FC 1511). That decision was **stayed pending appeal and overturned by the Federal Court of Appeal on January 30, 2026** (2026 FCA 17), which held the listing Order was a reasonable enabling measure that did not itself engage the criminal law power. **The Regulations remain in force.**
- **Still open:** leave to appeal to the SCC has been sought (file 42277), and a separate Federal Court challenge to the Regulations themselves (T-1468-22) is stayed pending final judgment on the Order.

### Federal Plastics Registry
- Producers who manufacture, import, or place plastic packaging, EEE, or single-use/disposable plastic products on the Canadian market must report annually under a CEPA s. 46 notice.
- **Phase 1 reporting continues** for the 2024, 2025 and 2026 calendar years. **Phases 2 and 3 were postponed** by an amended notice published March 14, 2026.
- Small producers placing under one tonne of plastic on the market are exempt.
- Failure to report, late reporting, or false/misleading information can attract CEPA ss. 272–273 penalties.

### Clean Fuel Regulations (SOR/2022-140)
- In force; last amended September 30, 2024. Impose declining carbon-intensity requirements on primary suppliers of gasoline and diesel, with minimum volumetric requirements (5% low-CI content in gasoline, 2% in diesel).
- **Targeted amendments** announced September 5, 2025 to support domestic low-carbon fuel production; consultation closed **January 15, 2026**. Draft amendments expected in Canada Gazette, Part I.
