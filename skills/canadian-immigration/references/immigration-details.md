# Canadian Immigration Deep Reference

## LMIA Application Checklist

### Before Applying
- [ ] Confirm position requires an LMIA (not LMIA-exempt under IMP)
- [ ] Verify occupation NOC code (National Occupational Classification)
- [ ] Determine wage: compare to provincial/territorial median wage to identify high-wage vs. low-wage stream
- [ ] Confirm employer is in good standing (no outstanding compliance violations)
- [ ] Prepare $1,000 processing fee per position (exemptions apply — see SKILL.md)

### Recruitment (must be completed before applying)
- [ ] Post on **Job Bank** (jobbank.gc.ca) — **4 consecutive weeks** (high-wage) or **8 consecutive weeks** (low-wage), within the 3 months before applying
- [ ] Enable and use **Job Match** (invite all matches rated 4★+ high-wage / 2★+ low-wage within the first 30 days) and **Direct Apply**
- [ ] Use at least **2 additional recruitment methods**:
  - High-wage: at least one must be **national in scope**; two online ads of the same type count as only one method
  - Low-wage: each must target a **different underrepresented group** (vulnerable youth, Indigenous peoples, newcomers, persons with disabilities, asylum claimants with valid work permits), **plus** separate youth-targeted recruitment (ages 15–30)
- [ ] Keep at least one recruitment activity **ongoing until the LMIA decision is issued**
- [ ] Document all recruitment activities: dates posted, platforms used, number of applicants, reasons for non-selection of Canadian applicants
- [ ] Retain all recruitment records for **6 years**

### Application Package
- [ ] LMIA application completed and submitted through **LMIA Online** (requires a Job Bank for Employers account)
- [ ] Job description (duties, requirements, wages, hours, benefits)
- [ ] Proof of recruitment efforts (screenshots, job postings, correspondence)
- [ ] Summary of Canadian/PR applicants reviewed and reasons for non-hire
- [ ] Transition plan (high-wage stream only)
- [ ] Business legitimacy documents (CRA business number, incorporation docs, financial statements if requested)
- [ ] Housing plan/documentation (agricultural streams)

### After Receiving Positive LMIA
- [ ] Provide foreign national with copy of LMIA confirmation letter (they need it to apply for work permit)
- [ ] Retain copy of LMIA and all supporting documents for 6 years
- [ ] Ensure work permit conditions match LMIA (occupation, wage, location, duration)

## CUSMA/USMCA Professional Categories (Selected)

US and Mexican citizens in the following occupations may work in Canada without an LMIA under CUSMA:

*Verify against IRCC's current CUSMA Appendix 2 list before relying on any entry.*

| Category | Minimum Credentials |
|----------|-------------------|
| Accountant | Baccalaureate or Licenciatura degree; or CPA, CA, CGA, or CMA |
| Computer Systems Analyst | Baccalaureate or Licenciatura degree; or Post-Secondary Diploma/Certificate + 3 years experience |
| Engineer | Baccalaureate or Licenciatura degree; or State/Provincial licence |
| Lawyer (including Notary in Quebec) | LL.B., J.D., LL.L., B.C.L., or Licenciatura degree; or membership in a provincial bar |
| Management Consultant | Baccalaureate or Licenciatura degree; or equivalent professional experience (5 years in consulting) |
| Scientist (various) | Baccalaureate or Licenciatura degree |
| Graphic Designer | Baccalaureate or Licenciatura degree; or Post-Secondary Diploma/Certificate + 3 years experience |
| Medical/Allied Professionals | Various (physician, dentist, pharmacist, etc.) — degree + licence |

**Application process:** CUSMA Professionals (LMIA exemption code **T36**), Traders (T34), Investors (T35) and Intra-Company Transferees (T37 executives/senior managers, T38 specialized knowledge) receive **employer-specific** work permits. The employer **must** submit an offer of employment through the **IRCC Employer Portal and pay the $230 employer compliance fee** before the worker applies — otherwise the work permit will be refused. Applicants arriving **from outside Canada** may apply at a port of entry; applicants **already in Canada** must generally apply online.

## IMP — Offer of Employment Steps

1. **Register** employer in the IRCC Employer Portal (one-time)
2. **Submit** an Offer of Employment for each foreign national — include:
   - Position details (NOC code, job title, duties, wage, hours)
   - Employment terms (start date, duration, location)
   - LMIA exemption code (codes were restructured 15 Dec 2022 — verify against IRCC's current
     exemption-code table). Common codes: C10 significant benefit (general), C11 entrepreneurs/
     self-employed, C61 ICT starting a branch/affiliate, C62 ICT executives/senior or functional
     managers, C63 ICT specialized knowledge, T34/T35/T36 CUSMA traders/investors/professionals,
     T37/T38 CUSMA ICT, C16 francophone mobility, C41 spouses of TEER 0–3 workers, C43 PGWP,
     A75 bridging open work permit.
3. **Pay** the $230 Employer Compliance Fee
4. **Receive** the Offer of Employment number
5. **Provide** the number to the foreign national — they include it in their work permit application
6. **Retain** all documentation for 6 years

## Employer Inspection Preparation Guide

ESDC inspections (TFWP) or IRCC inspections (IMP) can occur at any time within 6 years of the worker's first day of employment. Prepare a compliance binder:

### Records to Maintain
| Document | Retention Period |
|---------|----------------|
| LMIA and supporting documents | 6 years |
| Employment contracts (signed) | 6 years |
| Payroll records (pay stubs, T4s) | 6 years |
| Time and attendance records | 6 years |
| Housing documentation (if applicable) | 6 years |
| Health insurance records | 6 years |
| Transportation receipts (TFWP) | 6 years |
| Recruitment records | 6 years |

### Common Inspection Findings (Non-Compliance)
- Paying less than the committed wage (even by a small amount)
- Changing the worker's duties without amending the work permit
- Failing to provide health insurance during the waiting period
- Inadequate housing (agricultural workers)
- Retaining the worker's passport or documents (prohibited)
- Charging the worker for LMIA costs (prohibited — must be borne by employer)

### If Selected for Inspection
1. Notify HR and legal counsel immediately
2. Gather all records from the compliance binder
3. Be cooperative — obstruction worsens outcomes
4. Verify payroll records match committed wages for every pay period
5. Document any legitimate discrepancies proactively

## Work Permit Duration Guidelines

| Permit Type | Typical Duration |
|-------------|----------------|
| LMIA-based, high-wage stream | Up to 3 years (longer only in exceptional circumstances with rationale) |
| LMIA-based, low-wage stream | Maximum 1 year |
| CUSMA Professional (T36) | Up to 3 years, renewable — no cumulative maximum |
| ICT Executive/Senior Manager (C62 / T37) | Initial max 3 years (1 year for a new office); 2-year renewals; 7 years cumulative |
| ICT Specialized Knowledge (C63 / T38) | Initial max 3 years (1 year for a new office); 2-year renewals; 5 years cumulative |
| Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) | Program ≥2 years → 3 years; master's ≥8 months → 3 years; program ≥8 months but <2 years → up to program length. Capped by passport expiry. |
| Open Work Permit (spouse) | Tied to principal applicant's permit |

*An LMIA's 6-month validity is the deadline for the worker to **apply** for the work permit — it does not set the permit's duration, which IRCC determines from ESDC's recommended work duration.*

**Renewal:** The application must be received **before the current work permit expires** to preserve maintained status (online: before midnight UTC on the expiry date). IRCC **recommends** applying at least 30 days early, but the legal test is the expiry date. Under maintained status the worker may keep working under the **same** employer, occupation and location until a decision is made.

## Quebec (CAQ) requirements

For positions in Quebec lasting **more than 30 consecutive days**:
- LMIA must be submitted **simultaneously** to Service Canada and MIFI, in MIFI's required format
- Worker needs a **Certificat d'acceptation du Québec (CAQ)** in addition to the federal work permit. Temporary selection fee: **$233** (as of Jan 1, 2026; adjusted annually)
- No CAQ required if the work is 30 days or less, unpaid, LMIA-exempt, or work-permit-exempt
- **Level 4 spoken French** will be required for a CAQ after 3 years of work in Québec — enforcement begins **Dec 17, 2028** (agricultural workers and certain others exempt)
- Certain **Montréal and Laval** LMIA applications suspended to **Dec 31, 2026**, including facilitated-process and work-permit-renewal applications

## PGWP eligibility (changed Nov 2024)

- **Language:** CLB/NCLC **7** (university), CLB/NCLC **5** (college/polytechnic/non-university), for PGWP applications submitted on/after **Nov 1, 2024**
- **Field of study:** applicants whose **study permit application** was submitted on/after **Nov 1, 2024** and who graduated from a non-degree program must have graduated in an **eligible field of study** tied to occupations in long-term shortage. Bachelor's/master's/doctoral graduates exempt from the field-of-study requirement
- Flight school graduates exempt from both
- **Study permit cap context:** IRCC expects to issue up to **408,000** study permits in 2026 (437,000 in 2025; 485,000 in 2024), with **309,670** application spaces for PAL/TAL-required cohorts. Master's and doctoral students at public DLIs became **PAL/TAL-exempt Jan 1, 2026**. Quebec applicants use a **CAQ** instead of a PAL
