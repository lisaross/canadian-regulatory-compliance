# ESA Provincial Deep Reference: Overtime, Records & Vacation

## Overtime Pay Comparison

| Jurisdiction | Overtime Threshold | Overtime Rate |
|-------------|-------------------|--------------|
| Federal (CLC) | After 8 hrs/day or 40 hrs/week | 1.5× regular rate |
| Ontario (ESA) | After 44 hrs/week | 1.5× regular rate |
| BC (BCESA) | After 8 hrs/day or 40 hrs/week; after 12 hrs/day | 1.5× / 2× after 12 hrs |
| Alberta (ESC) | 8/44 rule — daily hours over 8 and weekly hours over 44 are **both** calculated for the week; the **greater** of the two totals is the number of overtime hours paid (they are not added together) | 1.5× regular rate |

**Overtime averaging:**
- **Ontario:** written or electronic agreement with the employee; averaging period **max 4 weeks**;
  must state a start and expiry date (expiry ≤2 years after start for employees not represented by
  a union). **Director approval has not been required since 3 April 2019** — nothing is filed.
- **Alberta:** an averaging **arrangement** may be **imposed by the employer** (consent not required)
  on **2 weeks' written notice**; period of **1 to 52 weeks**; must be in writing with a schedule of
  daily/weekly hours; beyond 52 weeks needs a Ministerial or Director's variance. Nothing is filed.
  Overtime is the greater of daily or averaging-period overtime.

## Vacation Entitlement

| Jurisdiction | Year 1–4 | Year 5+ | Pay Rate |
|-------------|---------|---------|---------|
| Federal | 2 weeks (after 1 yr) | 3 weeks (after 5 yrs); 4 weeks (after 10 yrs) | 4% / 6% / 8% of wages |
| Ontario | 2 weeks | 3 weeks (5+ yrs) | 4% / 6% |
| BC | 2 weeks | 3 weeks (5+ yrs) | 4% / 6% |
| Alberta | 2 weeks | 3 weeks (5+ yrs) | 4% / 6% |

## Statutory Holidays

### Ontario (9 public holidays)
New Year's Day, Family Day, Good Friday, Victoria Day, Canada Day, Labour Day, Thanksgiving Day, Christmas Day, Boxing Day.

**Public holiday pay formula:** Total regular wages + vacation pay earned in 4 work weeks before PH ÷ 20.

### BC (11 statutory holidays)
New Year's Day, Family Day, Good Friday, Victoria Day, Canada Day, B.C. Day, Labour Day,
National Day for Truth and Reconciliation (September 30), Thanksgiving Day,
Remembrance Day (November 11), Christmas Day.

**Note:** BC has **no Boxing Day** statutory holiday, and neither Easter Sunday nor Easter Monday
is a statutory holiday in BC.

### Alberta (9 public holidays)
New Year's Day, Alberta Family Day, Good Friday, Victoria Day, Canada Day, Labour Day, Thanksgiving Day, Remembrance Day, Christmas Day.

**Note:** In Alberta, an employee must have worked **30 workdays in the 12 months** before the
holiday to qualify. Entitlement is lost if the employee is absent on the last scheduled day before
or first scheduled day after the holiday **without the employer's consent**, or is absent when
scheduled to work the holiday itself. An employer-approved absence does not disqualify.

### Federal (10 general holidays — CLC s.166)
New Year's Day, Good Friday, Victoria Day, Canada Day, Labour Day, National Day for Truth and
Reconciliation (September 30), Thanksgiving Day, Remembrance Day, Christmas Day, Boxing Day.

**Note:** the federal list has **no Family Day**. Where New Year's Day, Canada Day, National Day for
Truth and Reconciliation, Remembrance Day, Christmas Day or Boxing Day falls on a non-scheduled
Saturday or Sunday, the employee gets the holiday with pay on the scheduled work day immediately
before or after.

## Record-Keeping Obligations

### Ontario (ESA ss.15, 15.1, 16)
Employers must retain:
- Hours worked each day and week — **3 years** after the day or week of work
- Wage statement information — **3 years** after it was given to the employee
- Employee name, address, start date — **3 years** after the employee stops working
- **Vacation time and vacation pay records — 5 years** after the record was made (s.15.1)
- Written/electronic agreements to work excess hours or average overtime — **3 years** after the
  last day work was performed under the agreement
- Disconnecting-from-work and electronic-monitoring policies — **3 years** after the policy ceases
  to be in effect

### BC (BCESA)
- Payroll records (s.28) must be retained for **4 years after the date each record was created** (s.28(2)(c))
- Time records showing start/end, breaks and **the hours worked on each day for every employee** —
  required **regardless of whether the employee is paid hourly, by salary, flat rate, piece rate,
  commission or other incentive basis** (s.28(1)(d))

### Alberta (ESC ss.14–15)
- Employment records must be kept for **at least 3 years from the date each record is made** (s.15)
  — not 3 years after employment ends
- Records must be available for inspection by an Employment Standards Officer

## Independent Contractor vs. Employee

The distinction matters for all employment standards obligations. Canadian courts apply a multi-factor test (no single factor is determinative):

| Factor | Points to Employment | Points to Contractor |
|--------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Control | Employer controls how work is done | Worker controls method |
| Ownership of tools | Employer provides tools | Worker owns tools |
| Chance of profit / risk of loss | Worker has no financial risk | Worker bears financial risk |
| Integration | Work integral to employer's business | Work is accessory |
| Exclusivity | Worker works only for one client | Worker has multiple clients |
| Written agreement | Employment agreement | Contractor agreement |

**Important:** A contract calling someone an "independent contractor" does not make them one. If the
economic reality is employment, ESA obligations apply. ESA **s.5.1(1)** prohibits an employer from
treating an employee as if they were not an employee, and misclassification can draw a notice of
contravention, prosecution, or both. **Note:** the "reverse onus" in the former s.5.1(2), which put
the burden on the employer to prove non-employee status, was **repealed effective 1 January 2019**
(Bill 47, Making Ontario Open for Business Act, 2018). The burden of proving employee status now
rests with the individual.

## Progressive Discipline Best Practices

Document all disciplinary steps to support a just cause defense:
1. Verbal warning (documented by manager)
2. Written warning (signed by employee)
3. Final written warning / suspension without pay
4. Termination for cause (with full documentation of all prior steps)

**Condonation:** Misconduct the employer knew of and took no action on is generally treated as
condoned and cannot later support a just cause termination. Act promptly on discovered misconduct.
(Common-law doctrine — no statutory limitation period applies.)
