# Canadian Employment & Labour Compliance

## Jurisdiction Map

The first question in any employment matter is: **which law applies?**

| Employer Type | Governing Legislation |
|--------------|----------------------|
| Federally regulated (banks, telecoms, airlines, interprovincial transport, broadcasting) | Canada Labour Code (CLC) |
| Ontario-based employer | Employment Standards Act, 2000 (ESA ON) |
| BC-based employer | Employment Standards Act BC (BCESA) |
| Alberta-based employer | Employment Standards Code AB (ESC AB) |

Federally regulated employers comprise roughly **6% of the Canadian workforce**. When uncertain, default to provincial law.

## Termination: Notice and Severance Requirements

### Federal (Canada Labour Code)
- **Notice (s.230):** Graduated by service — 2 weeks at 3 months; 3 weeks at 3 years;
  4 weeks at 4 years; 5 weeks at 5 years; 6 weeks at 6 years; 7 weeks at 7 years;
  8 weeks at 8+ years. Employer may give notice, wages in lieu, or a combination.
- **Severance pay (s.235):** Separate from and additional to notice. Owed to any employee with
  12+ consecutive months of continuous employment (unless dismissed for just cause): the
  **greater of** 2 days' wages per completed year of employment, or 5 days' wages.
- **Unjust dismissal:** Employees with 12+ months service and not covered by a collective agreement may file an unjust dismissal complaint (s.240 CLC). Remedy can include reinstatement.
- **Group termination (50+ employees at a single industrial establishment within 4 weeks, s.212):**
  16 weeks' written notice to the **Head of Compliance and Enforcement** (Labour Program), with
  copies immediately to the Minister of Employment and Social Development, the Canada Employment
  Insurance Commission, and any union (or posted for unrepresented employees). Employer must also
  establish a **Joint Planning Committee** — at least **4 members**, at least **half** appointed as
  representatives of the redundant employees (the rest employer representatives, ss.214–215);
  members must be appointed and hold their **first sitting within 2 weeks** of the s.212 notice. The
  committee develops an adjustment program, to be completed as expeditiously as possible. After
  **6 weeks** from the s.212 notice, all of the redundant-employee representatives, or all of the
  employer representatives, may agree and **apply jointly** to the Minister to appoint an arbitrator
  — either because the committee has not finished the program, or because it has and those members
  are not satisfied with it (s.223(1)).
  Employers must also give each affected employee a statement of benefits no later than 2 weeks
  before termination. Individual notice under s.230 still applies. Waivers may
  be requested from the Minister of Labour; a waiver does not affect severance rights.

### Ontario (ESA, 2000 — Part XV)
- **Individual notice (s.57):** Based on length of service:

| Years of Service | Minimum Notice |
|-----------------|---------------|
| Less than 1 year | 1 week |
| 1–3 years | 2 weeks |
| 3–4 years | 3 weeks |
| 4–5 years | 4 weeks |
| 5–6 years | 5 weeks |
| 6–7 years | 6 weeks |
| 7–8 years | 7 weeks |
| 8+ years | 8 weeks |

- **Severance pay (s.64):** Applies where the employee has **5+ years** of employment **and** the
  employer **either** has a global payroll of at least **$2.5M** **or** severed the employment of
  **50 or more employees in a six-month period because all or part of the business permanently
  closed**. Calculation: regular wages for a regular work week × (completed years + completed
  months ÷ 12), to a maximum of 26 weeks.
- **Termination pay in lieu:** Employer may pay wages instead of working notice.
- **Mass termination (50+ employees at one establishment within 4 weeks):** Notice is based on the
  number terminated, not individual service — 8 weeks (50–199), 12 weeks (200–499), 16 weeks (500+).
  Employer must file **Form 1** with the Director of Employment Standards; **notice to employees is
  not effective until the Director receives Form 1**. On the first day of the notice period the
  employer must post Form 1 and give a copy to each affected employee — and, since **1 July 2025**,
  must also give each affected employee the most recent version of the ministry's **Employment
  Ontario Career Supports information sheet**. Since 26 Oct 2023,
  "establishment" **includes the home of an employee who works exclusively remotely**, so remote
  workers count toward the 50. Affected employees are also entitled to **job seeking leave** — up to
  3 unpaid, **job-protected** days during the notice period to engage in activities related to
  finding employment (ESA s.50.3, since 27 Nov 2025). The leave does **not** apply where the employee
  is given notice that is **25% or less** of the required notice period and receives the remainder as
  termination pay in lieu.

### BC (Employment Standards Act BC)
- **Notice:** Based on service:

| Years of Service | Minimum Notice |
|-----------------|---------------|
| 3–12 months | 1 week |
| 1–3 years | 2 weeks |
| 3–4 years | 3 weeks |
| 4–5 years | 4 weeks |
| 5–6 years | 5 weeks |
| 6–7 years | 6 weeks |
| 7–8 years | 7 weeks |
| 8+ years | 8 weeks |

- **Group termination (50+ at a single location within 2 months, s.64):** 8 weeks (50–100),
  12 weeks (101–300), 16 weeks (301+). Written notice to each affected employee, the
  **Minister of Labour**, and any trade union. In addition to individual compensation for length
  of service under s.63.

### Alberta (Employment Standards Code)
- **Notice:** Based on service:

| Years of Service | Minimum Notice |
|-----------------|---------------|
| 90 days – 2 years | 1 week |
| 2–4 years | 2 weeks |
| 4–6 years | 4 weeks |
| 6–8 years | 5 weeks |
| 8–10 years | 6 weeks |
| 10+ years | 8 weeks |

- **No statutory severance** pay in Alberta (unlike Ontario).
- **Group termination:** 50+ employees at a single location within a 4-week period requires at least
  **4 weeks' written notice to the Minister** responsible for Employment Standards, in addition to
  individual termination notice.

### Just Cause Dismissal
Statutory minimum notice is **not required** if the dismissal is for just cause. Just cause is a high bar — typically includes serious misconduct, theft, fraud, persistent insubordination, or repeated performance issues despite progressive discipline. Document everything. When in doubt, provide notice or pay in lieu to reduce wrongful dismissal risk.

## Human Rights — Canadian Human Rights Act (Federal) & Provincial Codes

### Protected Grounds
Under the **CHRA (federal)**, employers cannot discriminate based on:
Race, national/ethnic origin, colour, religion, age, sex, sexual orientation, gender
identity/expression, marital status, family status, **genetic characteristics**, disability, and
conviction for an offence for which a pardon has been granted **or in respect of which a record
suspension has been ordered** (s.3(1)).

Provincial codes have their own, non-identical lists. Ontario's Human Rights Code, for example, adds
ancestry, place of origin, creed, receipt of public assistance and **record of offences**, and does not
list genetic characteristics. Check the applicable code — do not assume the federal grounds carry over.

### Duty to Accommodate
Employers have a **duty to accommodate** employees with a disability (or other protected ground) to the point of **undue hardship**. Undue hardship is assessed considering: cost, health and safety risks, and (for larger employers) the financial ability to bear costs.

Steps:
1. Employee discloses a need for accommodation.
2. Employer requests sufficient medical/supporting documentation.
3. Parties collaborate to identify workable accommodation options.
4. Employer implements the solution unless undue hardship is demonstrated.
5. Document all steps and decisions.

### Harassment
Employers have an obligation to maintain a harassment-free workplace. Federal employers (CLC Part II) must have a workplace harassment and violence prevention policy. Ontario employers (OHSA) must have a workplace harassment policy and annual review. Failure to investigate complaints promptly is itself a violation.

## Leaves of Absence

### Federal (Canada Labour Code)
| Leave | Duration | Key Rule |
|-------|----------|---------|
| Maternity | 17 weeks | Unpaid; job-protected |
| Parental | 63 weeks (standard) / 71 weeks (extended) | Unpaid; job-protected |
| Personal leave | 5 days | First 3 paid after 3 months' service; cannot be used to treat own illness/injury since Dec 2022 |
| Medical leave with pay | 10 days paid/year | Accrues 3 days after 30-day qualifying period, then 1 day/month; carryover up to 10 |
| Pregnancy loss | 8 weeks (stillbirth) / 3 days (other) | First 3 days paid at 3 months' service; in force 12 Dec 2025 |
| Bereavement — death of a child | Up to 8 weeks | First 3 days paid; in force 12 Dec 2025 |
| Bereavement | 10 days (3 paid) | |
| Family violence | 10 days (5 paid) | |

### Ontario (ESA, 2000)
| Leave | Duration |
|-------|----------|
| Pregnancy | Up to 17 weeks |
| Parental | Up to 61 weeks (birth parent who took pregnancy leave); 63 weeks (all other new parents) |
| Family medical | Up to 28 weeks in 52-week period |
| Sick leave | 3 days unpaid (employer **cannot require a medical note** as of 28 Oct 2024) |
| Bereavement | 2 days unpaid |
| Long-term illness | Up to 27 weeks unpaid in a 52-week period (in force 19 Jun 2025) |
| Job seeking | Up to 3 days unpaid during a mass-termination notice period (in force 27 Nov 2025) |
| Domestic or sexual violence | Up to 10 days + 15 weeks per calendar year; **first 5 days paid** |

## Pay Equity

**Federal Pay Equity Act (in force 2021):** Proactive regime for federally regulated employers with 10+ employees. Employers must establish and maintain a pay equity plan, identify wage gaps, and close them over time. Pay equity committees required for 100+ employees.

**Ontario Pay Equity Act:** Requires equal pay for work of equal value in public and broader public sector; private sector employers with 10+ employees must also comply.

**Quebec Pay Equity Act:** Applies to employers with 10+ employees; proactive obligation to establish and maintain pay equity.

## Minimum Wage (verified 2026-09-04)

| Jurisdiction | General Minimum Wage | Effective | Next scheduled change |
|-------------|---------------------|-----------|----------------------|
| Federal | $18.15/hr | 1 Apr 2026 | 1 Apr 2027 (annual CPI indexation) |
| Ontario | $17.60/hr | 1 Oct 2025 | **$17.95/hr on 1 Oct 2026** (announced) |
| BC | $18.25/hr | 1 Jun 2026 | 1 Jun 2027 (annual BC CPI indexation) |
| Alberta | $15.00/hr | 1 Oct 2018 | none scheduled |
| Quebec | $16.60/hr | 1 May 2026 | 1 May 2027 |

Federally regulated employers must pay the **higher** of the federal rate and the rate of the
province/territory where the employee works. These change annually on fixed dates (federal 1 Apr,
Quebec 1 May, BC 1 Jun, Ontario 1 Oct). Always verify before advising.

## Recent changes to verify

- Ontario job posting rules (ESA Part III.1, O. Reg. 476/24; 25+ employees) — in force 1 Jan 2026:
  publicly advertised postings must state the expected **compensation or a compensation range**
  (subject to the regulation's exceptions), disclose **use of artificial intelligence where AI is
  used to screen, assess or select applicants**, state whether the posting is for an **existing
  vacancy**, and omit any **Canadian experience** requirement. Employers must inform each applicant
  they **interviewed** whether a hiring decision has been made, within **45 days** of the last
  interview.
- BC Pay Transparency Act — postings must state pay range; reporting (50+ employees) due by 1 Nov 2026.
- Ontario disconnecting-from-work policy (25+ employees) — due by 1 March.
- Ontario electronic monitoring policy (25+ employees) — due by 1 March.
- Ontario non-compete ban (ESA Part XV.1) — in force 25 Oct 2021.
- Federal workplace harassment and violence prevention policy — joint review every 3 years; aggregate report to Labour Program due by 1 March.
