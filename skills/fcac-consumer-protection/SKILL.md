# FCAC — Financial Consumer Agency of Canada

## Overview

The **Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC)** is the federal regulator responsible for protecting the rights and interests of consumers of financial products and services offered by **federally regulated financial institutions (FRFIs)** — primarily chartered banks.

FCAC enforces the consumer provisions of the **Bank Act**, **Insurance Companies Act**, **Trust and Loan Companies Act**, and **FCAC Act**. It does not regulate provincial credit unions, insurance brokers, or payday lenders — those fall under provincial jurisdiction.

**FCAC powers:** Investigate complaints → Issue compliance agreements → Publication of enforcement outcomes — under FCAC Act **s.31(1)** the Commissioner **shall**, subject
to any regulations, make public the nature of a violation, the name of the person who committed it
and the penalty imposed; regulations made under s.19(1)(c.1) may prescribe circumstances in which the
name is **not** published, and the Commissioner has withheld names in practice → Impose administrative monetary penalties up to **$10 million per violation** for a **financial institution
or a payment card network operator** ($1 million for a natural person), under FCAC Act s.19(2) (ceiling in
force since April 30, 2020). Actual penalties are set using FCAC's published AMP Framework, which bands amounts by harm, negligence/intent and violation history — $10M is a statutory ceiling, not a typical outcome.

## Core Consumer Rights Under the Bank Act

### 1. Right to Open a Retail Deposit Account
Under **Bank Act s.627.17**, a member bank must open a retail deposit account for any individual requesting one in person at a branch or point of service where such accounts are opened, and who presents:

- **two documents from a reliable source** — one showing name and address, the other name and date of birth. The statutory list includes: identification issued by the Government of Canada or a province; recent notices of tax assessment; recent statements of government benefits; recent Canadian public utility bills; recent bank account or credit card statements; foreign passports; **or**
- **one reliable-source document** showing name and date of birth, if identity is also confirmed by a customer in good standing with the bank or a person of good standing in the community.

The list is deliberately broader than photo ID — a utility bill plus a benefits statement qualifies. FCAC's February 2023 bulletin on non-standard ID directs banks to have policies that actively support access rather than defaulting to refusal.

- Banks **cannot** require a minimum deposit or minimum balance
- Banks **cannot** require a credit check, employment, or funds on hand
- Bankruptcy is not a ground for refusal

**Grounds on which a bank may refuse (s.627.18):**
- reasonable grounds to believe the account will be used for illegal or fraudulent purposes
- a history of illegal or fraudulent activity **in relation to financial service providers** in the past 7 years (a history of activity, not a criminal conviction)
- reasonable belief the person knowingly made a false statement in the information given
- reasonable belief the person may cause physical harm to, harass, or abuse other customers or employees
- the person refuses to allow verification of the identification presented
- the bank offers only accounts linked to an existing account elsewhere and the person has none
- for a federal credit union, refusal to become a member on request

Any refusal requires a **written statement**, plus disclosure of the complaint procedure, OBSI's contact information, and FCAC's address, website and phone number.

### 2. Cheque Hold Periods
The **Access to Funds Regulations (SOR/2012-24)** cap hold periods on cheques deposited to a retail deposit account or an eligible enterprise account:

**Covered instruments (s.2):** the hold caps and the first-$100 rule apply only to **paper-based** cheques or
other instruments deposited in Canada that are **MICR-encoded**, **not damaged or mutilated** so as to be
unreadable by cheque clearing systems, **drawn on a branch located in Canada**, and **issued in Canadian
dollars**. Instruments outside this definition — including any not in Canadian dollars — fall outside ss.3
and 4 entirely; they are not "exceptions."

| Cheque amount | In person with an employee | Any other way (ATM, mobile) |
|---|---|---|
| $1,500 or less | 4 business days after the day of deposit | 5 business days after the day of deposit |
| More than $1,500 | 7 business days after the day of deposit | 8 business days after the day of deposit |

**First $100:** the first **$100 of the aggregate** of all funds deposited by cheque or other instrument to a
retail deposit account **on any one day** must be available **immediately** if deposited in person with an
employee at a branch or point of service, or **on the business day following the deposit** if deposited any
other way (s.4). This is one $100 per account per day across all instruments deposited that day — not $100
per cheque.

**Exceptions to ss.3 and 4 (s.6(1)):** reasonable grounds to believe the deposit is being made for illegal or
fraudulent purposes in relation to the depositor's account; account open less than 90 days; cheque endorsed
more than once; cheque deposited at least six months after its date. An institution relying on s.6 to refuse
compliance must give the depositor **written notice** of the refusal and of their right to contact FCAC —
immediately if deposited in person with an employee, or on request if deposited any other way (s.6(2)).

**Separate exception (s.5):** for a deposit by an **eligible enterprise**, **s.3 alone** (not the s.4
first-$100 rule) does not apply where the institution has reasonable grounds to believe there is a material
increased credit risk.

**Free cashing of Government of Canada cheques:** since June 30, 2022, any bank must cash one up to **$1,750** free, even for non-customers, on acceptable ID (raised from $1,500).

### 3. NSF (Non-Sufficient Funds) Fees
**Since March 12, 2026**, NSF fees on personal deposit accounts are capped by regulation — Regulations Amending the Financial Consumer Protection Framework Regulations (SOR/2025-96):

- Maximum **$10 per NSF occurrence** (down from the $45–$50 charged before)
- **No more than one NSF fee within 2 business days** for the same personal deposit account
- **No NSF fee at all** where the account is in unauthorized overdraft by **less than $10**

Scope: personal and joint deposit accounts at Schedule I and II banks and authorized foreign banks (~79 institutions). Does **not** apply to business or corporate accounts, provincially regulated credit unions, or NSF-type fees charged by merchants or non-bank lenders.

Note: the Canada Gazette Part I draft (Nov 16, 2024) would have required a pre-fee electronic alert plus a 3-hour grace period. **That requirement was dropped from the final regulations** and is not in force.

Banks must still disclose NSF fee amounts in fee disclosure documents at account opening and in annual fee disclosure.

### 4. Cancellation Rights (Cooling-Off Periods)

| Agreement type | Cancellation window |
|---|---|
| Ongoing product/service by mail or telephone | until end of the **14th business day** (s.627.1(1)(a)) |
| Ongoing product/service any other way | until end of the **3rd business day** (s.627.1(1)(b)) |
| Retail deposit account | notify within **14 business days** of opening; closed without charge, non-interest charges related to operating the account refunded (FCPF Regs s.5(2)(b)) |
| Deposit account other than retail | **14 business days** (FCPF Regs s.3) |
| Deposit-type instrument (e.g. GIC) | notify within **10 business days** of issuance (FCPF Regs s.6) |
| Optional product or service | notify at any time; effective at end of billing cycle or **30 days** after notice, whichever is earlier, pro-rated refund (FCPF Regs s.7) |

For **general ongoing agreements under s.627.1** — that is, products and services **other than** the
prescribed products governed by s.627.11 — timely cancellation obliges the bank to waive any cancellation
charge, acknowledge in writing without delay, and refund amounts received, recovering only amounts related to
actual use and reasonably incurred expenses (s.627.1(2)–(3)). This does **not** displace the product-specific
rules in the table above: retail deposit accounts, deposit-type instruments, optional products and the other
prescribed products in FCPF Regs s.4 are governed by s.627.11(2) and FCPF Regs ss.5–8 instead.

There is **no** 30-day right to cancel a credit card after use.

## Cost of Borrowing — Disclosure Requirements

The **Cost of Borrowing (Banks) Regulations (SOR/2001-101) were repealed effective June 29, 2022**. Bank cost-of-borrowing disclosure now sits in **Bank Act Part XII.2 (ss. 627.57, 627.89 and following)** and the **Financial Consumer Protection Framework Regulations (SOR/2021-181)**, in force June 30, 2022. Cost of Borrowing Regulations still apply to trust and loan companies and insurance companies (SOR/2001-104 and equivalents) — cite the right instrument for the institution type.

The following disclosure requirements apply under this framework:

### Credit Cards
**Before account opening (pre-contractual disclosure):**
- Annual interest rate (purchase, cash advance, balance transfer — separately)
- Annual fee
- Grace period (minimum days before interest accrues on purchases)
- Minimum payment formula
- Any balance protection or insurance product offered

**Monthly statement requirements:**
- Opening and closing balance
- Transactions listed with dates
- Interest charged (separately for purchases, cash advances, balance transfers)
- Minimum payment amount **and** the date by which it must be paid
- The estimated time, in months and years, to pay the outstanding balance in full if only the minimum payment is made (FCPF Regs s.66(1)(e))

**Note:** the statement must disclose the **time to payoff**, not the total interest cost of minimum payments. Not required where the agreement requires the balance to be paid in full each month.

### Mortgages
**Before signing:**
- Total mortgage amount
- Amortization period
- Payment frequency and amount
- Interest rate and type (fixed/variable)
- Term
- **Prepayment privileges** — what the borrower is permitted to prepay without penalty
- **Prepayment penalty** — how it is calculated if the borrower exceeds privileges or breaks the mortgage

**After signing:** Under the voluntary **Code of Conduct for Federally Regulated Financial
Institutions: Mortgage Prepayment Information**, adhering FRFIs provide an annual statement showing
prepayment privileges, the dollar amount prepayable without charge, how the prepayment charge is
calculated with examples, and customized figures valid as of the statement date. This is a code
commitment, not a Bank Act requirement.

### Personal Loans and Lines of Credit
- Annual percentage rate (APR) or annual interest rate
- Total amount of credit
- Total cost of borrowing (if calculable)
- Payment schedule
- Any conditions under which the rate may change

## Mortgage Prepayment Rights

**Important:** Bank Act s.627.28(3) gives a statutory penalty-free prepayment right for fixed-amount consumer loans, but **s.627.28(2)(a) expressly excludes loans secured by a mortgage on real property**. There is no legislated minimum prepayment privilege for mortgages — the common 10–20% annual lump-sum allowance is **contractual**.

What is legally required is **disclosure**:
- Prepayment privileges and charges in a single, prominently displayed **information box at the beginning of the mortgage agreement**
- The **manner in which the charge is calculated** and a **description of each component** of the formula, in language that is clear, simple and not misleading (Commissioner Guidance **CG-9**, in effect since March 2013)
- Where the calculation is inherently complex, a **simplified calculation, example or illustration**

Under the voluntary **Code of Conduct for FRFIs: Mortgage prepayment information** (CBA members), lenders additionally post a prepayment calculator publicly; staff a toll-free line where staff give the **actual current charge orally** and a **written statement on request** — there is no statutory turnaround such as "3 business days"; and on confirmed prepayment provide a written statement giving the charge, how it was calculated (months' interest vs IRD), the outstanding amount, the mortgage rate, the comparison rate, the remaining term used, the quote's validity period, factors that could change it, and any other amounts payable.

### Prepayment Penalty Calculation Methods

**Interest Rate Differential (IRD):**
Penalty ≈ Remaining principal × (Contract rate − Comparison rate for equivalent remaining term) × Remaining months ÷ 12

**3 Months' Interest:**
Penalty = Remaining principal × Annual interest rate × 3 ÷ 12

Most fixed-rate mortgage contracts specify the **greater of** the two, and the comparison rate used (posted vs discounted) varies materially by lender. This is a contractual convention, **not** a Bank Act or FCAC requirement — read the specific agreement.

### FCAC Guideline on Existing Consumer Mortgage Loans in Exceptional Circumstances
Published and effective **July 5, 2023**. Sets FCAC's expectations that FRFIs provide **tailored relief** to consumers with a mortgage on their principal residence facing severe financial stress and at risk of default, including proactive contact with at-risk accounts. FRFIs report **quarterly** to FCAC on at-risk accounts, proactive contact, and relief measures.

## Complaint Handling — Tiered Process

All federally regulated banks must have a **complaint handling process** that meets FCAC standards:

### Tier 1 — Bank's Internal Process (currently in force)
1. The bank must treat **any expression of dissatisfaction** with a product or service, or with how it was sold or provided, as a complaint — through any channel
2. Written acknowledgement of the date received, **without delay** (no 5-business-day standard)
3. If not dealt with within **14 calendar days**, escalate internally to a **designated employee** with the experience, competence and authority for complex complaints
4. Deal with the complaint within **56 calendar days** of the day received (s.627.43; FCPF Regs s.14). **The 56-day period may not be paused for any reason.** Where exceeded for reasons beyond the bank's control, document and record the reason
5. Issue a final written response stating the decision reached and the consumer's right to escalate to the ECB

**From June 23, 2027 (revised Guideline):** published **June 23, 2026**, implementation date **June 23, 2027**. The step-2 acknowledgement becomes a formally titled **Notice of Acknowledgement**, and the step-5 final response becomes a **Notice of Final Decision** — titled as such, stating it concludes the internal process, with the date first communicated, time taken, statement of facts with timelines, the decision and how it was reached, and the method used to determine redress. The Guideline also shifts "reimbursement" to **"remediation" on a harm basis**, and adds detailed **ECB information-sharing duties**.

### Tier 2 — External Complaints Body (ECB)
**Since November 1, 2024, OBSI is Canada's single, sole ECB for all federally regulated banks.** The Minister of Finance designated OBSI in October 2023 following an FCAC-led application process; the multiple-ECB model ended. The six banks that used ADRBO (RBC, TD, Scotiabank, National Bank, Tangerine, Digital Commerce Bank) transferred to OBSI. **ADRBO no longer acts as an ECB.**

A consumer may escalate at the **earlier of** the 56-day period elapsing or receipt of the Notice of Final Decision, and has **180 days** from the final response to do so. OBSI must advise in writing within **30 calendar days** if a complaint falls outside its terms of reference, and make a **final written recommendation within 120 calendar days** of receiving the information needed. Recommendations are **not binding**; maximum recommended compensation is **$350,000** (as of 2026 — the 2026 Independent External Review recommended $550,000 with inflation indexing plus binding authority; not adopted). OBSI must publish a free summary 90 days after a final recommendation.

### Tier 3 — FCAC
FCAC does not resolve individual disputes but investigates whether the bank followed its own complaint process and complied with the Bank Act consumer provisions. Consumers can file a complaint at **fcac-acfc.gc.ca**.

## Credit Card Specific Protections

| Protection | Requirement |
|-----------|------------|
| Grace period | Minimum **21 days** interest-free on new purchases if previous balance paid in full |
| Over-limit transactions | Banks must obtain **express consent** before allowing over-limit transactions; must disclose any over-limit fee |
| Balance protection insurance | Disclosure of total cost required; opt-in consent (not negative option billing) |
| Unsolicited credit limit increases | **Prohibited** — express consent required for **each individual increase**, at the time of the increase. Credit Business Practices Regulations s.6; FCAC Commissioner Guidance **CG-5** holds that blanket or "evergreen" pre-consent does **not** satisfy express consent. Using the card is not consent. |
| Summary of rights | Banks must provide a summary of credit card rights to every new cardholder |

## Plain Language Requirements

- All disclosure required under the Bank Act and FCPF Regulations must be made in language, and presented in a manner, that is **clear, simple and not misleading**
- FCAC publishes **Clear Language and Presentation Principles and Guidelines** to assist institutions
- FCAC's revised Complaint-Handling Guideline adds accessibility expectations for complaint channels and consumer communications

## The Financial Consumer Protection Framework (in force June 30, 2022)

**Bank Act Part XII.2** and the **FCPF Regulations (SOR/2021-181)** introduced more than 60 new and enhanced consumer measures. Beyond complaint handling:

**Appropriateness.** Banks must offer and sell products appropriate for the consumer's needs, collect and assess information to understand those needs, and tell the consumer if a product is assessed as not appropriate. Compensation must not interfere with this duty.

**Express consent per product.** Required for **each** product, in writing or verbally with written confirmation, together with a copy of the agreement. Banks may **no longer** obtain consent for multiple products in a single agreement. Use of a product is not consent.

**Separate agreements for optional products,** stating description, term, all fees, cancellation conditions, availability date, and steps to use.

**Electronic alerts.** Without delay when a chequing/savings balance falls below **$100** (or a consumer-set amount), and when available credit on a card or line of credit falls below **$100**. Automatic — no sign-up required. The duty does not apply where the consumer has opted out **in writing**, or where the consumer has **not provided the contact information** required to receive the alert (s.627.13(2)); FCAC expects banks to *proactively solicit* that contact information rather than wait to be contacted. Not for business accounts.

**Renewal reminders (s.627.6).** For a product or service provided for a term of more than 30 days: **21 days**
and **5 days** before the last day of the term. Term of 30 days or less: one reminder **5 days** before.
Excludes **business-purpose** agreements and **loans secured by a mortgage on real property**.

**Promotional-offer reminders (s.627.61).** For a promotional, preferential, introductory or special offer
lasting more than 30 days: **21 days** and **5 days** before the last day of the offer period. Period of
30 days or less: one reminder **5 days** before. Excludes **business-purpose** acceptances and **optional
products or services**.

**Undue pressure and misleading information.** Prohibited from taking advantage, applying undue pressure or coercion for any purpose — broader than the former coercive tied selling prohibition. The false/misleading information prohibition extends to all bank communications, to consumers, the public and FCAC.

**Redress.** Refund or credit fees or penalties, with interest, where the charge was not disclosed or disclosed incorrectly, or where express consent was absent.

**Whistleblowing.** Mandatory program handling employee reports of wrongdoing and prohibiting reprisal. Employees may report to the bank, FCAC, OSFI, or law enforcement.

**Regulatory reporting.** Complaint records retained **7 years**; complaint data filed **quarterly, within 60 days of quarter-end**; public accountability statements within **135 days** of financial year end; reportable compliance issues (>120 days to fix, or >250 consumers affected, or ongoing >1 year before detection) within **60 days** of being reported to compliance.

## Adjacent Regimes to Flag

**Code of Conduct for the Payment Card Industry in Canada** — effective **October 30, 2024** (balance April 30, 2025), replacing the 2015 Credit and Debit Card Code. Governs PCNOs (Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Interac, Discover, UnionPay), acquirers and downstream participants. Merchant complaints **acknowledged within 5 business days**, **investigated and addressed within 20 business days** (30 for PCNOs), semi-annual reporting to FCAC. A **merchant**-facing regime — do not confuse its 5-day acknowledgement with bank consumer complaint handling.

**Criminal interest rate** — since **January 1, 2025**, Criminal Code s.347 sets the rate at **35% APR** (down from 60% EAR ≈ 48% APR); the basis moved from EAR to APR. Offering or advertising credit above the rate is now itself an offence. Criminal Interest Rate Regulations (SOR/2024-114) cap payday loan cost of borrowing at **$14 per $100** and exempt commercial loans over $500,000 (no cap), commercial loans $10,000–$500,000 (48% APR), and pawn loans under $1,000 (48% APR).

**Retail Payment Activities Act** — registration provisions in force **November 1, 2024**; PSPs had to apply by November 15, 2024. Bank of Canada published the PSP registry and the risk-management and funds-safeguarding requirements came into force **September 8, 2025**. Registration fee $2,500 (indexed). Supervised by the **Bank of Canada**, not FCAC.

**Consumer-Driven Banking Act** — the 2024 first tranche was **repealed and replaced** by a new Act via Bill C-15, Royal Assent **March 26, 2026** (S.C. 2026, c. 3, s. 224). Oversight moved from **FCAC to the Bank of Canada**, aligning with its RPAA role. Draft regulations pre-published in Canada Gazette Part I on **June 27, 2026**. Phase 1 is **read access only**; phase 2 (write access / payment initiation) targeted **mid-2027**, contingent on Real-Time Rail. AMPs up to **$10M** for participating entities, $1M for individuals. The screen-scraping prohibition is enacted but **not yet in force**. The Minister may designate provincial regulators for specified provisions.

**Commitment on Low-Cost and No-Cost Accounts** — modernized Commitment effective **December 1, 2025** (replacing 2014), **14 signatories** including the six largest banks. Low-cost chequing at **no more than $4/month**, **at least 18 debit transactions** (up 50%), no minimum balance, free digital statements, no switching fee. **$0/month** mandatory for youth, students, GIS-receiving seniors, RDSP beneficiaries and **newcomers in their first year**, plus at least one of: Indigenous peoples, Disability Tax Credit recipients, or recipients of certain provincial/territorial social assistance.
