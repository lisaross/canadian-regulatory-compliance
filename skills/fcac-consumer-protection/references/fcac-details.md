# FCAC Consumer Protection Deep Reference

## Complaint Handling Compliance Checklist

Use this checklist to assess whether your bank's complaint handling process meets FCAC requirements:

### Written Policy
- [ ] Written complaint handling policy published on website and available in branch
- [ ] Policy covers all products and services offered to retail customers
- [ ] Policy available in English and French
- [ ] Policy reviewed and updated at least annually

### Intake and Acknowledgement
- [ ] Multiple channels: in person, phone, mail, online
- [ ] Dedicated email address or online form
- [ ] Any expression of dissatisfaction with a product/service or how it was sold treated as a complaint
- [ ] Written acknowledgement (Notice of Acknowledgement) sent **without delay** — not on a business-day count
- [ ] Acknowledgement includes: date received, overview of the process including internal escalation, actions and timelines (including the 56-day prescribed period), and how/when the consumer may contact OBSI

### Investigation and Response
- [ ] Escalated to a **designated employee** if not dealt with within **14 calendar days**
- [ ] Consumer offered the option to escalate to a designated employee before the 14 days elapse
- [ ] Complaint dealt with within **56 calendar days** of the day received
- [ ] The 56-day period is **never paused** — no internal process, holiday, or information request stops the clock
- [ ] Where exceeded for reasons beyond the bank's control, the reason is documented and recorded internally
- [ ] **Notice of Final Decision** issued: titled as such, states it concludes the internal process, includes date first communicated, time taken, statement of facts with timelines, the decision and how it was reached (including financial and non-financial harm), method used to determine redress and remediation, and the right to escalate to OBSI
- [ ] Comprehensive redress policy covering timely remediation, including recurring or systemic issues
- [ ] Complaint records retained a minimum of **7 years**

### Referral to External Body
- [ ] Final response includes name and contact information of **OBSI** — Canada's single ECB since November 1, 2024
- [ ] Consumer informed of the right to escalate to OBSI at no cost, at the earlier of 56 days elapsing or the Notice of Final Decision
- [ ] Bank is a member in good standing of OBSI (mandatory for all federally regulated banks)
- [ ] Process to provide OBSI, without delay, with all relevant information on notified complaints — contracts, agreements, internal and consumer correspondence, transaction records, investigation documents
- [ ] Complaint data submitted to FCAC **quarterly, within 60 days after the end of each quarter**
- [ ] Complaint-handling procedures filed with FCAC, published on website, available as a branch brochure, and provided in writing on request
- [ ] Required FCAC annex text included in online and print complaint-handling information

### Prohibited Practices
- [ ] No fee charged to customers for submitting a complaint
- [ ] No requirement for customers to waive rights as a condition of complaint resolution
- [ ] No retaliation against customers who file complaints (e.g., account closure without valid reason)

## Credit Card Disclosure Template Review

When reviewing a credit card agreement or disclosure document, check for these required elements:

**Key Disclosure Box (must appear prominently at the front):**
- [ ] Annual interest rate — purchases
- [ ] Annual interest rate — cash advances (often higher)
- [ ] Annual interest rate — balance transfers
- [ ] Annual fee (if any)
- [ ] Grace period (minimum 21 days)
- [ ] Minimum payment formula (e.g., greater of $10 or 2% of balance)

**Statement Requirements:**
- [ ] Minimum payment amount shown
- [ ] Payment due date shown
- [ ] "Minimum payment warning" box: estimated time in months and years to pay off the balance at minimum payments (FCPF Regs s.66(1)(e)) — total interest cost is **not** a prescribed element
- [ ] Interest charges broken out by transaction type
- [ ] Credit limit and available credit shown

**Common Deficiencies Found by FCAC:**
- Interest rate on cash advances not disclosed separately from purchases
- Grace period disclosed as "up to 21 days" rather than exact number of days
- Minimum payment warning missing or incomplete
- Balance protection insurance not clearly identified as optional
- Annual fee buried in fine print rather than in key disclosure box

## Prepayment Penalty Worked Examples

*Illustrative — the "greater of" rule is a typical contractual term, not a legal requirement.*

### Example 1 — IRD Penalty (Fixed Rate, Rates Have Fallen)

**Scenario:**
- Original mortgage: $500,000 at 5.89% fixed, 5-year term
- Breaking mortgage with 2.5 years remaining
- Current posted rate for 2.5-year term: 4.19%

**Calculation:**
- IRD rate = 5.89% − 4.19% = 1.70%
- Remaining principal: $462,000 (after payments)
- Penalty = $462,000 × 1.70% × (30 months ÷ 12) = **$19,635**

**3 months' interest alternative:**
- $462,000 × 5.89% × (3 ÷ 12) = $6,802

**Penalty = higher of IRD vs. 3 months' interest = $19,635**

### Example 2 — IRD Penalty (Rates Have Risen — IRD May Be Lower)

**Scenario:**
- Same mortgage at 5.89%, 2.5 years remaining
- Current posted rate for 2.5-year term: 6.50% (rates have risen)

**Calculation:**
- IRD rate = 5.89% − 6.50% = **negative** → IRD = $0 (no differential to charge)

**Penalty = higher of $0 vs. 3 months' interest = $6,802**

**Key insight:** When rates rise after you take out a mortgage, you typically pay only 3 months' interest to break it. When rates fall, the IRD can be very significant.

## NSF Fee Compliance Guide

### Statutory Cap (in force March 12, 2026)
SOR/2025-96 added s.10.1 to the FCPF Regulations:
- **$10 maximum** NSF fee where a natural person has insufficient funds in a personal deposit account
- **No NSF fee more than once within 2 business days** in respect of any of that person's accounts
- **No NSF fee at all** where the account is in unauthorized overdraft by **less than $10**

Registered March 12, 2025; in force on the first anniversary of registration, after industry raised IT-system concerns about the originally proposed staggered timeline.

### What is NOT in force
The Canada Gazette Part I draft (Nov 16, 2024) also proposed a pre-fee **electronic alert** between 8am and 4pm local time with a **minimum 3-hour grace period**, and annual public disclosure of NSF fee volumes and revenue. **These did not make it into the final regulations.** Do not advise that a notice or grace period is required.

There is **no** FCAC voluntary NSF code, **no** $25 cap, **no** 12-hour notice right, and **no** "one free NSF per year" entitlement.

### Scope
- Applies to: Schedule I and II banks and authorized foreign banks (~79 institutions), personal and jointly held personal deposit accounts
- Does **not** apply to: business or corporate accounts, provincially regulated credit unions, or NSF-type fees charged by merchants or non-bank lenders

### Continuing bank obligations
- Disclose the NSF fee at account opening and in annual fee disclosure
- Give written information about any new charge or fee increase before it takes effect
- Send the mandatory **electronic low-balance alert** at $100 or a consumer-set amount — a separate and independent obligation from the NSF regime

### Related affordability measures
- **Modernized Commitment on Low-Cost and No-Cost Accounts**, in force December 1, 2025: $4/month maximum, ≥18 debit transactions, no minimum balance; $0/month for youth, students, GIS-receiving seniors, RDSP beneficiaries and newcomers in their first year, plus one further group chosen by each signatory. 14 signatories.
- The government has directed FCAC to publish a report on the structure, level and transparency of Canadian bank fees during 2026.

## Right to Basic Banking — Refusal Scenarios

Banks frequently struggle with the line between legitimate refusal grounds and discrimination. FCAC guidance clarifies:

**Legitimate refusal grounds (Bank Act s.627.18):**
- A **history of illegal or fraudulent activity in relation to financial service providers** within the past 7 years — a history of activity, not a criminal conviction; the statute does not require the bank to produce a criminal record
- Reasonable belief the person knowingly made a false statement in the information given
- Reasonable grounds to believe the account will be used for illegal or fraudulent purposes
- Reasonable belief the person may cause physical harm to, harass, or abuse other customers or employees
- The person refuses to allow verification of the identification presented
- The bank offers only accounts linked to an existing account at another bank, and the person has none
- For a federal credit union, refusal to become a member on request

Any refusal requires a **written statement**, plus the complaint procedure, OBSI's contact information, and FCAC's address, website and phone number.

**NOT legitimate refusal grounds:**
- No fixed address
- Unemployment, or no money to deposit at opening
- Prior bankruptcy
- Low credit score, or no credit history
- Non-citizen or non-resident status, where acceptable documents are presented
- Race, ethnicity, or any other prohibited ground of discrimination

**Required ID (s.627.17(1)):** **two documents from a reliable source** — one showing name and address, the other name and date of birth — from a list including: identification issued by the Government of Canada or a province; recent notices of tax assessment (federal, provincial or municipal); recent statements of government benefits; recent Canadian public utility bills; recent bank account or credit card statements; foreign passports. Alternatively, **one** reliable-source document showing name and date of birth suffices if identity is confirmed by a customer in good standing with the bank or a person of good standing in the community.

This is **not** a photo-ID list — a utility bill plus a benefits statement qualifies. FCAC's February 2023 bulletin on non-standard ID directs banks to operationalize "reliable source," "customer in good standing" and "person of good standing in the community" in a way that actively supports access. Where the bank has reasonable grounds to suspect misrepresentation of identity, it may additionally require one piece of federal or provincial photo-and-signature ID (s.627.17(1)(c)).

## FCAC Administrative Monetary Penalties

**Statutory ceiling (FCAC Act s.19(2), in force April 30, 2020):**
- **$10,000,000** for a violation by a financial institution or payment card network operator
- **$1,000,000** for a violation by a natural person

There is **no per-violation-type tariff** — $10M is a single ceiling applying to any violation of a consumer provision.

**How FCAC sets the amount** (published AMP Framework, FCAC Act s.20 criteria):

| Factor | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harm (incl. duration) | Some: $0–2,000,000 | Significant: $2,000,000–4,000,000 | Very significant: $4,000,000–6,000,000 |
| Negligence / intent | Some: $0–500,000 | Significant: $500,000–1,000,000 | Very significant or intent: $1,000,000–2,000,000 |
| Violation history | Little/none: $0–500,000 | Significant: $500,000–1,000,000 | Very significant: $1,000,000–2,000,000 |

FCAC must also consider duration and ability to pay. The stated purpose is to **promote compliance**, not to punish.

**Typical violation categories:** failure to provide required cost-of-borrowing disclosure; charging a fee not disclosed in the fee disclosure document; increasing a credit limit without express consent; negative-option billing; failure to maintain a compliant complaint process; failure to refer a consumer to OBSI; providing false or misleading information.

**Publication (s.31(1)).** Since April 30, 2020 the Commissioner **shall**, *subject to any
regulations*, make public the nature of a violation, the name of the person who committed it and the
amount of the penalty. Regulations under s.19(1)(c.1) may prescribe circumstances in which the name
is not published, and the Commissioner weighs factors including egregiousness, acceptance of
responsibility, consumer impact, deterrence, collaboration and remediation — names have been withheld
on that basis (e.g. Commissioner's Decision #125, September 2025). Reputational consequences
routinely exceed the monetary penalty.

**Practical note.** Actual AMPs have been well below the ceiling. In February 2026 the Federal Court set aside a $1.6M AMP in part because the Commissioner applied the post-2020 $10M ceiling retrospectively to conduct that was 95% pre-amendment. Pre-2020 ceilings were $200,000 (Jan 1, 2010 – May 23, 2012) and $500,000 (May 24, 2012 – Apr 19, 2020).
